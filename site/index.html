How a sale works

Five steps, each in writing. Nobody at your firm, no board and no client hears from us without your written permission.

  1. A private first call

    No documents. You decide whether to go further.

    Week one

  2. Numbers under NDA

    We sign a mutual confidentiality agreement, then you share two years of statements and your management agreements.

    When you are ready

  3. An indication of value

    A price range and deal shape, explained line by line.

    Usually within two weeks

  4. Letter of intent

    Price, structure, your role, your staff and timing, before anything is binding. We also show you how the deal is funded.

    When we agree

  5. Review and closing

    An independent accountant confirms the numbers behind the offer. We show the funds are committed, then close.

    Usually 60 to 90 days after the letter

Questions owners ask us

Have you bought a firm before?

Ryeton is new, and our first acquisition is under way. On the first call we will tell you who we are and exactly where we stand.

Do my staff keep their jobs?

That is our plan. Your managers know your buildings and boards, and that is much of what we buy. Any change is written into the letter of intent.

When do my boards and clients find out?

When you decide, on a plan we agree. If a contract needs a board’s consent, we plan that step together.

How do you set the price?

On your earnings after a market salary for the owner, checked by an independent accountant, and on your contracts. We explain the range line by line.

Asset sale or stock sale?

Either can work, depending on how your firm is set up and whether your management agreements can be assigned. It changes your taxes, so bring your accountant and lawyer early.

Who answers for work that software prepares?

Your manager. Software drafts and prepares; a person reviews, approves and remains responsible. Urgent and sensitive matters always go to a person.

Who funds the deal, and what kind of owner will you be?

Ryeton is founder-led and funded by private investors. With the letter of intent we explain how your deal is funded, and before closing we show the funds are committed. We buy to own and run firms for the long term, with no timetable to resell.

What if the deal does not happen?

Your numbers stay under the NDA and are returned or destroyed. Nobody at your firm or among your clients hears from us.

What does it cost me?

Nothing on our side. You pay your own lawyer and accountant.
